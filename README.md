# Dissecting the Cellular Source of Ki-67 Proliferation in Glioma

## 📌 Abstract

The nuclear protein Ki-67 serves as the standard clinical marker for estimating tumor proliferation and grading gliomas. However, current histopathological assessment lacks lineage resolution and potentially conflates tumor intrinsic growth with microenvironmental expansion.

**Here we dissect the cellular sources of Ki-67 expression using a single-cell transcriptomic atlas of 40 glioma patients.** We demonstrate a fundamental divergence in proliferative architecture where glioblastoma proliferation is tumor-dominated while low-grade glioma proliferation frequently originates from non-neoplastic myeloid and glial compartments.

We identify four distinct proliferative ecotypes designated as:
* **Tumor-dominant**
* **Immune-dominant**
* **Glial-dominant**
* **Stromal-dominant**

These ecotypes are governed by lineage-specific transcriptional metaprograms. Crucially, we resolve a clinical paradox in high-grade gliomas: among patients with uniformly high Ki-67 indices, those with immune-dominant proliferation driven by tissue-resident microglia exhibit significantly superior survival compared to those with tumor-dominant proliferation. **These findings indicate that aggregate Ki-67 indices mask biologically distinct states and suggest that resolving the cellular source of proliferation is essential for accurate prognostication.**
